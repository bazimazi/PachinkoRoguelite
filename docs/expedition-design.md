# Expedition expansion: implementation and validation

PLUMB v0.4 preserves the original active pachinko identity: aim matters, nudges are scarce, the gate is a commitment, and each sphere has a different intervention. The expansion increases decisions and combinations across expeditions. It does not lengthen falls to manufacture playtime.

## What changed and why

The v0.3 presentation pass rebuilds the hub as an observatory with an animated sphere specimen and separate expedition console. Illustrated artifact cards, connected fork diagrams, an inspectable constellation route, an operator field guide, and a refined cockpit replace the earlier largely unchanged presentation. Static machine relief is cached; conservatory, foundry, gravity, and boss boards have separate architectural identities. The launcher marks the first predicted contact, and an optional whole-machine overview exposes hazards and landing pockets before release. The boss displays five physical seals; sphere contrast, trails, chain timing, and reactor charge remain visible during play. Ordinary status notifications are compact; major celebrations have a separate treatment.

Overdrive adds a timing choice within every machine. Five distinct physical peg contacts charge a reactor. Activation gives a bounded aimed lift, doubles direct collision earnings, and holds ordinary combo decay for three seconds. Each machine permits two activations. Repeated contact and relic lightning cannot farm charge; hazards still damage the sphere and break its combo. Landing payouts and passive relic earnings are excluded from the doubling. Eleven dedicated checks plus 168 seeded actual falls verify these boundaries. Four additional controller checks verify real-physics charging, keyboard/button parity, and clearing queued abilities across pause paths.

The original route contained five or six machines and only three guaranteed drafts. The new seeded graph contains three acts, ten required machines, an optional eleventh, seven forks, three choice events, and two full-repair act transitions. Safe paths preserve shell and earn spending money; several risk branches offer additional relic drafts. Six board anomalies change physics or scoring geometry. Routes, board generation, offers, events, and shop rerolls use separate seeded streams.

The v0.4 pass adds two optional guardian types to existing rooms rather than extending the route. The Act I Bellkeeper installs three physical bells in the gallery and kiln: each can be credited once, in any order, and retracts afterward. Completion awards 70 gears. The Act II Heartforge in the dynamo requires four approaching physical contacts in the order I → II → I → II and awards 120 gears. Wrong contacts do not reset progress, but do not advance it. Relic lightning, echo effects, and resting overlaps cannot forge objective credit. Bounties are flat, once-only awards; Overdrive, board payout, and economy engines cannot multiply them. A completed guardian opens one relic draft if the player survives the machine. The risky kiln's original relic remains guaranteed on survival even without completion; finishing its bells adds the bounty rather than another relic draft.

Each unfinished guardian grants one automatic return when the sphere enters an original non-hazard collector before 75 seconds of simulation time, with shell remaining. A shield does not turn a hazard gutter into an eligible collector. The return preserves objective progress, elapsed time, shell, earnings, multiplier state, and remaining resources; it neither refills controls nor pays a premature landing bonus. After that one return, after the time window, or after objective completion, collectors settle the machine normally. Gutters retain ordinary shield/damage behavior. An unfinished objective never blocks progression after a surviving drop. This gives a second approach without creating an endless scoring loop or requiring every player to complete a side objective.

The persistent guardian instrument names the action, marks actual credited Bellkeeper IDs or the next Heartforge step, and shows bounty and remaining safe return. The journal pauses play and explains the complete rules. The phone header prioritizes the action and progress, with additional reserved playfield space. Live notifications use compact edge panels so credit and returns remain visible without covering the sphere.

Screenshot review also moved first-contact captions away from launcher geometry, removed redundant combo-name and chain-lightning captions, and capped nearby payout feedback while retaining its actual earnings. Phone readiness and guardian completion use the persistent instruments instead of collector-covering notices. Nudge and shared trick/tilt recovery now disable their buttons temporarily and expose recovery state, matching the simulation's existing cooldowns.

Contextual Workshop coaching follows the real first-room state: choose and release a first contact, try a nudge, then charge and activate Overdrive. Steps can change when Overdrive becomes ready; completing both an actual nudge and activation saves completion. Gold Rudder nudge refunds cannot hide a successful nudge. Fresh profiles receive tips automatically, while existing profiles with completed runs are treated as experienced during migration. The tips can be dismissed, and How to play can enable them again for the next Workshop without replacing a stored expedition. Desktop instructions retain full explanations; narrow phone tips use concise action headings and two-line copy. Sphere tricks remain documented in the field guide rather than being part of this three-step coach.

The catalogue grows from ten to eighteen relics and from ten to fourteen upgrades. Every new relic has an observable trigger and a bounded payoff. Lightning cannot trigger itself or collect precision effects; investments emit negative currency events; gold recovery requires a timely hit on a previously unrewarded gold peg. The economy tests reconcile every simulation event against drop earnings. Pool selection excludes owned items and still offers opportunities to pivot.

A free starting relic creates a plan before the first drop. Three seeded commissions offer intermediate skill goals and recovery. Full healing between acts gives fragile builds room to develop. Shop prices scale by act, while repairs compete with new parts for the same gears. Research unlocks additional choices instead of permanent base-stat advantages; its costs rise as the archive grows.

The initial boss sweep produced zero passive wins in 117 attempts. Its old pocket rule ended a run despite remaining shell. Missed pockets now cost shell and relaunch with retained core progress; five genuine strikes remain necessary. The unupgraded boss regression verifies that every sphere has a winning launch. This is a reachability check, not an intended win-rate target or a substitute for player skill testing.

## Replay and interruption contract

- Each sphere unlocks Trials 0–6 independently. Cumulative rules alter moving pegs, nudge reserves, prices, gate width, shell capacity, and anomaly coverage. The hub explains the selected rule.
- A boss victory offers an explicit stopping point. Continuing preserves the build and increases circuit pressures within bounded limits. Circuit victories retain their challenge and mastery credit after a subsequent defeat.
- Daily constellations share a UTC date boundary, fixed sphere, and full research archive. Scores are local. Replays are allowed, with no missed-day penalty.
- Shared links capture the rules version, seed, sphere, trial, mode, and research catalogue. New expeditions use rules 3. Rules 2 links, checkpoints, and old logbook records retain the original guardian-free route; a missing rules field on an old checkpoint or record means rules 2. Imported rules 2 challenges and their journals identify the classic route. Guardians are installed only after the original route random stream has finished, and consume no new random draws. Individual decisions and input timing remain part of the challenge. Future incompatible changes must preserve or explicitly version this contract.
- New daily personal bests use a rules 3 date key; old rules 2 scores remain separate. A replay uses the rules version captured in its record, rather than silently adopting new objectives.
- Checkpoints store decision boundaries. Mid-drop reloads replay the machine from its saved start, preventing partial earnings from being counted twice. Purchases, rerolls, rewards, events, and victory decisions are checkpointed. Malformed checkpoints are rejected without deleting the archive.
- The journal pauses the machine and shows build effects, commission progress, and the route. Settings preserve the previous live state. Tab hiding clears pending and held controls before pausing. Seed editing and native button activation do not also launch the game.

## Automated evidence and its limits

`npm run build` typechecks the production source and produces the static game. `npm test` covers actual relic behavior, finite resource caps, payout accounting, deterministic replay, guardian target identity and order, one safe return, hazard/timeout boundaries, route reachability, anomalous physics, DOM interactions, save migration, checkpoint restoration, daily/replay contracts, and complete simulated expeditions. `npm run verify:encounters` isolates the guardian mechanics and real-physics reachability checks. `npm run verify:visuals` renders desktop and phone Canvas frames and checks that drawing never changes gameplay.

The route suite evaluates 144 generated expeditions, 9,216 complete graph paths, and 1,305 actual anomalous falls, including fully populated builds under high trial/circuit pressure. The integration suite runs real simulation through all ten required machines with each sphere, using legal launch selection, earned resources, recovery, and actual rewards. These demonstrate functioning systems and reachable victories. They do not establish the right difficulty, run duration, audience satisfaction, or long-term balance.

The first pass used native Canvas images and DOM/controller tests. The v0.3 pass adds a reusable Playwright suite in desktop Chromium, touch-emulated phone/tablet Chromium, and desktop Firefox. It exercises real clicks and taps, physics-driven reactor charging and activation, pause/resume, seed editing, settings, safe-boundary continuation, drafts, forks, events, shops, repairs, bargains, victory, machine identities, large text, reduced motion, and layout fit. Screenshots and failure traces are retained under `artifacts/`. Browser audio quality, physical touch ergonomics, target-device performance, and player enjoyment still need observed device playtesting. Gamepad support, remappable controls, localization, cloud saves, online leaderboards, platform achievements, and mod support are future production work; they are not claimed features.

Final v0.4 verification passes the production build, 23 build/physics checks, 11 Overdrive checks across 168 falls, 12 guardian checks across 720 falls, 32 expedition integration checks, 112 Canvas renders, and all 40 Playwright scenarios. The browser run uses `PLUMB_TEST_LIGHT=1`: screenshots remain enabled while trace and video recording are disabled. Guardian scenarios use real physical contacts and legal launch input through completion, survival, and the earned relic draft. They also verify finite-return instructions, live objective layout, coaching completion through actual controls, dismissal and replay, and classic-route compatibility. Existing checks include control hit targets, readable mobile action labels, non-overlapping headers, cooldown readiness, and finite nudge/trick/tilt/gate spending. Later decision screens use saved checkpoint fixtures; full physical victories run in the expedition harness. Screenshot review confirmed readable phone coaching and guardian states and resolved launcher-label overlap, duplicate feedback over the sphere, and collector-covering readiness notices.

## The next evidence to collect

Use the protocol and primary sources in [genre-research.md](genre-research.md). Start with small observed novice and experienced-player groups. Watch whether players understand their first relic, act on Workshop tips, distinguish safe returns from gutters, read the next relay correctly, explain the boss, discover a second build, resume successfully, and voluntarily begin another expedition. Measure decision time and actual run duration before choosing pacing targets. Audit loss causes, branch choice, purchases declined, and complete builds across spheres and trials. A more specific loss debrief and an isolated practice laboratory remain proposals, not implemented features. Revise content from these observations before adding more systems.

Commercial success requires real audience feedback, a stable release, clear positioning, a representative trailer, and platform work. This expansion is an implemented and tested foundation for that process; no test here proves virality or world-class quality.
